# Teh and Huang 1980: Fibre Orientation In Composite-Beam Vibration

- PDF: `docs/literature/pdf/teh_huang_1980_fibre_orientation_composite_beams.pdf`
- Citation key: `teh_huang_1980_fibre_orientation_composite_beams`
- Bibliographic form: K. K. Teh and C. C. Huang, "The effects of fibre orientation on free vibrations of composite beams", Journal of Sound and Vibration 69(2), 327--337 (1980), doi:10.1016/0022-460X(80)90616-1.
- Role for CoupledBeams: closest early source for asking how the spectrum and mode shapes change with reinforcing-fibre orientation.

## What Matters For The Project

- The paper varies the reinforcing-fibre orientation of a cantilevered generally orthotropic graphite/epoxy beam and studies natural frequencies, bending--twisting interaction and mode shapes.
- It reports that the influence of fibre orientation depends on mode order and that mode shapes can change rapidly over small orientation intervals.
- The bending moment induced by twisting and the twisting moment induced by bending are examined separately, which supports careful physical interpretation of coupled modes.

## Terminology And Notation

- Important terms: fibre-orientation angle, torsion--flexure coupling, predominantly flexural mode, predominantly torsional mode, bending moment induced by twisting and twisting moment induced by bending.
- The paper uses a cantilever-beam coordinate system and normalized mode-shape amplitudes; these are not the branch identity or joint notation of CoupledBeams.

## Critical Places In The Paper

- pp. 327--328: abstract and introduction state the orientation and mode-shape questions.
- Sec. 2: Hamilton-principle formulation, bending/twisting moments, equations of motion and boundary conditions.
- Fig. 2: first eight nondimensional natural frequencies versus fibre orientation.
- Figs. 3--4: the two directions of bending--twisting influence versus orientation and mode order.
- Figs. 5--11 and Sec. 4: changes in normalized mode shapes and the paper's conclusions about the most sensitive angle range.

## Mismatch With The Current Model

- The geometry is one cantilevered beam, not two rods joined at an angle.
- The paper does not derive the current rigid-joint conditions or use the branch-continuation convention of this repository.
- Its reported angle intervals and material values are source-specific and are not thresholds for the submitted article.

## Use As Source

Use for the qualitative and historical claim that fibre orientation can change the spectrum, bending--torsion balance and mode shapes of a composite beam. Do not use it as the current two-arm frequency equation.
