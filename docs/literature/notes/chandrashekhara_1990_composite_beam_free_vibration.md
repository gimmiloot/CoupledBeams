# Chandrashekhara et al. 1990: Composite-Beam Free Vibration

- PDF: `docs/literature/pdf/chandrashekhara_1990_composite_beam_free_vibration.pdf`
- Citation key: `chandrashekhara_1990_composite_beam_free_vibration`
- Bibliographic form: K. Chandrashekhara, K. Krishnamurthy, and S. Roy, "Free vibration of composite beams including rotary inertia and shear deformation", Composite Structures 14(4), 269--279 (1990), doi:10.1016/0263-8223(90)90010-C.
- Role for CoupledBeams: source for the importance of transverse shear and section rotary inertia in laminated composite beams.

## What Matters For The Project

- The paper gives exact free-vibration solutions for symmetrically laminated composite beams using first-order shear deformation and rotary inertia.
- It states that the solution procedure accommodates arbitrary boundary conditions.
- Its comparisons isolate effects of shear deformation, material anisotropy and boundary conditions on natural frequencies.
- This supports the general motivation for using a refined Timoshenko-type bending model for composite beams.

## Terminology And Notation

- Important terms: first-order shear deformation, rotary inertia, symmetrically laminated beam, stress resultants and arbitrary boundary conditions.
- The displacement field uses mid-plane axial and transverse displacements together with rotation of the normal; those variables are local to this laminated-beam formulation.

## Critical Places In The Paper

- pp. 269--270: abstract and introduction explain why transverse shear is important for composites.
- pp. 270--273: first-order shear-deformation kinematics, constitutive resultants and equations used for free vibration.
- The exact-solution and boundary-condition construction that follows the formulation.
- The numerical tables and discussion near the end of the paper comparing shear, anisotropy and end-condition effects.

## Mismatch With The Current Model

- The formulation is for symmetrically laminated bending beams and is not a source for Voigt--Lekhnitskii generalized torsion.
- It does not include the current rigid angular joint between two rods.
- Its exact solution should not replace the verified Chapter-2 equations or determinant structure.

## Use As Source

Use to justify considering transverse shear and rotary inertia in composite-beam vibration and to describe the scope of first-order shear-deformation models. Do not cite it for generalized torsion or current joint conditions.
