# Miller and Adams 1975: Orthotropic-Beam Resonances

- PDF: `docs/literature/pdf/miller_1975_orthotropic_beam_resonances.pdf`
- Citation key: `miller_1975_orthotropic_beam_resonances`
- Bibliographic form: A. K. Miller and D. F. Adams, "An analytic means of determining the flexural and torsional resonant frequencies of generally orthotropic beams", Journal of Sound and Vibration 41(4), 433--449 (1975), doi:10.1016/S0022-460X(75)80107-6.
- Role for CoupledBeams: early source for material flexure--torsion coupling in a generally orthotropic beam; literature background rather than a joint model.

## What Matters For The Project

- The paper treats flexural and torsional resonances together when the coupling is produced by the elastic constants of a generally orthotropic material.
- It explicitly connects the off-axis orientation of continuous reinforcing filaments with changes in the elastic constants and in the resonance spectrum.
- The characteristic condition admits both flexural and torsional resonances, so the paper is useful historical support for treating the lower spectrum as coupled rather than as a purely bending sequence.
- Its numerical example compares the analytic predictions with previously published experimental values.

## Terminology And Notation

- Important terms: `generally orthotropic beam`, flexural resonance, torsional resonance, flexural--torsional coupling, fibre-orientation angle and characteristic matrix.
- The paper's coordinates, elastic constants and circular-frequency notation belong to its single-beam formulation and should not replace the established project notation.

## Critical Places In The Paper

- pp. 433--435: abstract and introduction define the material origin of flexural--torsional coupling and distinguish generally orthotropic from transversely isotropic beams.
- pp. 435--443: analytic formulation and the solution branches used to construct the resonance condition.
- pp. 443--447: end conditions, characteristic matrix, determinant search and numerical example.
- pp. 447--448: discussion of the sensitivity of flexural and torsional resonance ordering to material constants and the comparison with experiment.

## Mismatch With The Current Model

- The object is one straight beam, not two rectangular rods connected through a rigid angular joint.
- The paper does not supply the current joint compatibility/equilibrium conditions or the submitted article's frequency determinant.
- Its material and beam assumptions must not be substituted for the verified Yartsev Chapter-2 source line without a separate theory audit.

## Use As Source

Use for the historical claim that material anisotropy can couple flexural and torsional resonances and alter their spectral ordering. Do not cite it as the derivation of the current rigid-joint model.
